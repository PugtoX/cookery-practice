Eight people · one bench each

A small cooking school in Sydney

We teach cooking the way it is learned at home — by doing it, badly at first, with someone standing next to you who can see what went wrong.

Why eight people

Eight is the largest group where everyone can hold a knife at the same time without waiting for a bench. It also means we can see what each person is doing and correct it in the moment, which is the only part of learning to cook that a video cannot do.

Larger classes are cheaper to run and worse to be in. We are not going to run them.

How we teach

  • We cook alongside you rather than demonstrating from the front.
  • We explain the reason behind a step, so you can apply it to a different recipe.
  • We tell you when something is good enough, because most home cooking fails from over-correcting.
  • We keep the equipment ordinary: nothing in class needs a machine you do not own.

Every class ends with a sit-down meal. Cooking something and then not eating it is a demonstration, not a class.

Where we are

We are in the city, a short walk from the station. The kitchen is a working one — we cook in it every week, and it is set up for eight people to work at once.

Who teaches

Classes are taught by people who have cooked professionally and then chose to teach, rather than by presenters reading a script. Every teacher has run a commercial kitchen service within the last five years.

If you want to know who is teaching a specific date before you book, ask us and we will tell you.
